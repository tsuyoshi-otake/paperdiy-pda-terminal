## Does the lid stay shut?

The two HG-MF08 are free-stop friction hinges: together 0.157 N·m (0.118..0.196 with their ±25 %). The lid side's moment about the axis is at most 0.066 N·m (level, 138 g at 49.0 mm), so the hinges hold it at any angle (1.8x at least) and it never falls shut by itself.

Let go, friction hinges turn back a little (the twist stored in them and in their seats). Anywhere else that is made up for by pushing a little further; at the closed position the lid lands on the tray and cannot go further, so it comes up again: every degree at the axis is 1.7 mm at the front edge. The snap is what keeps it down.

| | at the front edge |
| --- | --- |
| push from the hinges' spring-back, less the lid's weight | 0.5..1.3 N |
| the snap holds (plate model, 0.30 mm engagement) | 13.9 N |
| the snap holds if the print leaves 0.15 mm | 6.9 N |
| to open: snap + hinge friction - the lid's weight | about 14.8 N |

**The plate model overstates the hold.** The earlier snap (a 12 mm lip with a 35 deg catch face, 5.7 N by the same model) let go by itself in a print: the real wall is softer than the model's (the ceiling it hangs from gives, too) and a 0.45 mm bump prints rounder than drawn. Read the figures above as an upper bound, by a rough estimate two to five times the real hold. This snap (30 mm lip, 45 deg) holds 2.4 times what the earlier one did by the model. The snap coupons of plate 0 take in the whole flex zone: click them together and pull them apart before printing the lid and the tray. Too hard to open: file the bump's upper flank a little.

Press the front of the lid until the snap clicks; a lid that is only laid down stays open by the spring-back. To open, hold the base: it weighs 64 g plus the keyboard, less than the pull on the lid, and the hinges would carry it up with the lid unless the keyboard weighed about 332 g.
