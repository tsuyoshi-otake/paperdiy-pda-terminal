## Does it tip over backwards?

## Does the lid stay shut?

The two HG-MF08 are free-stop friction hinges: together 0.157 N·m (0.118..0.196 with their ±25 %). The lid side's moment about the axis is at most 0.066 N·m (level, 138 g at 49.1 mm), so the hinges hold it at any angle (1.8x at least) and it never falls shut by itself.

Let go, friction hinges turn back a little (the twist stored in them and in their seats). Anywhere else that is made up for by pushing a little further; at the closed position the lid lands on the tray and cannot go further, so it comes up again: every degree at the axis is 1.7 mm at the front edge. The snap is what keeps it down.

**The rims near the hinges.** Reported from the built device: with the hinge screws tight the lid opens, with the screws loose it stays shut. The hinge axis lies in the parting plane, so rims that touch 1.65 mm from it and come out 0.05 mm proud hold the front edge 3 mm open once the screws pull the leaves home. The lid's underside therefore leans up toward the hinges (`PART_RELIEF`, 0.4 mm at the back face). How much of the lid's coming up was this rather than spring-back is not known; if most of it, the snap had far more than the push below to hold, which would be why it never held.

The snap's figures are simulated: `tools/snap_sim.py` closes the built lid on the built tray along the hinge's arc, with the lip as a spring (plate model) and friction 0.3 on the ramps; all cases are in `out/snap_sim.md`.

| | at the front edge |
| --- | --- |
| push from the hinges' spring-back, less the lid's weight | 0.5..1.3 N |
| to close (the lid 0.2 mm forward .. where it was drawn) | 4..13 N |
| the snap holds (the same two cases) | 7..17 N |
| the snap holds if the lip's printed gap is 0.10 mm wider than drawn | 12 N |
| the snap holds with the lid 0.4 mm forward | 0 N (the bump does not reach its groove) |
| to open: snap + hinge friction - the lid's weight | about 8..18 N |

**No stops.** For one revision (never printed) two tongues on the lid reached down inside the tray's front wall to keep the lid from shifting forward. They are gone: the levering at the hinges is the likelier reason the earlier snaps did not hold; with a lid that sits forward and does not give they would have ridden up on the tray's wall; and they could not be put where the lip is. What remains true: every 0.1 mm the lid sits forward of its drawn position takes 0.1 mm off the engagement (0.37 mm on the built parts). If the closed lid's front face stands proud of the tray's by more than about 0.2 mm, measure the step: the lip has to be moved by that much.

The forces are the plate model's (ceiling and end walls rigid), so the real ones are somewhat lower. They are higher than the 8 N to close and 10 N to open quoted before: those were worked out for 30 and 35 degree faces, and the bump as built has 44 and 49 degree faces (`src/build.py`, `latch_flanks`). An earlier note that the model overstates the hold several times over was drawn from snaps that never engaged, and does not stand. The snap coupons of plate 0 take in the whole flex zone: click them together and pull them apart before printing the lid and the tray. Too hard to open: file the bump's upper flank a little.

Press the front of the lid until the snap clicks; a lid that is only laid down stays open by the spring-back. To open, hold the base: it weighs 64 g plus the keyboard, less than the pull on the lid, and the hinges would carry it up with the lid unless the keyboard weighed about 332 g.
